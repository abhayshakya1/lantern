## Key Question 1: Reported business segments (Global Consumer/Private Banking, Global Wholesale Banking, Global Markets, Insurance, Others): scope, products, financials FY24/FY25/1H26/9M26, share of profit, drivers, executive in charge

### Takeaway
OCBC reports four operating segments plus "Others". Global Wholesale Banking is the largest profit engine (FY25 PBT S$3.59bn, 39% of Group PBT), followed by Global Consumer/Private Banking (S$2.21bn, 24%), Insurance/GEH (S$1.49bn, 16%) and Global Markets (S$948m, 10%); in 1H26 Insurance and Consumer/Private Banking grew fastest (+43% and +26% YoY) on a wealth-management and investment-income boom, while in 3Q26 GEH's mark-to-market losses dragged Group profit even as "Banking Operations" profit hit a record S$1.90bn.

### Inferences
- Because treasury income sold to consumer and corporate customers is booked in the customer segments, Global Markets' reported PBT understates the economic footprint of the markets franchise (customer-flow trading income was a record S$1.35bn in 9M26, most of which sits in GCPB/GWB). Any "markets/forecasting" proposal therefore has stakeholders in all three banking segments, not only Global Markets.
- The Insurance segment is the most volatile line (3Q26 GEH contribution fell to S$147m from S$471m in 2Q26 on bond mark-to-market), which is why OCBC increasingly reports "Banking Operations" separately.
- With no named successor as Head of Global Wholesale Banking, the sub-business heads (Corporate, Commercial, Investment Banking, Transaction Banking, FI) appear to report directly to the Group CEO (inference from the management-team page; not explicitly confirmed by OCBC).

### Gaps
- OCBC does not publish quarterly segment PBT (only half-yearly), so 9M26/3Q26 segment profit is unavailable; the 9M26 pie-chart percentages are from text extraction of slide labels and the exact pairing of 9M26 vs 9M25 labels is ambiguous.
- Exact composition of "Others" (property, investment holding, Bank of Ningbo share) is not broken out.

## Key Question 2: Bank of Singapore — AUM over time, client segments, markets, CIO office, platforms, headcount, strategy, CEO

### Takeaway
Bank of Singapore (BoS), OCBC's wholly-owned private bank, grew AUM by more than 20% in 2025 to surpass its US$145bn target early (from roughly US$120bn when CEO Jason Moo took over in early 2023), runs on Singapore, Hong Kong and Dubai hubs (plus Luxembourg and Malaysia wealth units), has ~500 relationship managers, and is pushing an AI-heavy platform agenda (RM Copilot, HOLMES AI, agentic Source-of-Wealth Assistant) with an ambition to be a top-five Asian private bank within three to five years.
