## Key question 1: Capital and prudential regime (D-SIB status, Basel III final reforms, minimum ratios, buffers, leverage, LCR/NSFR, TLAC) and OCBC's current ratios

### Takeaway
OCBC is one of seven MAS-designated D-SIBs and must hold CET1/Tier 1/Total CAR of at least 6.5%/8.0%/10.0% plus a 2.5-point capital conservation buffer (9.0%/10.5%/12.5% all-in). At 30 September 2026 it reported CET1 of 15.5% on the transitional final-Basel-III basis and 14.0% fully phased-in (its own 14% target), a leverage ratio of 6.5%, all-currency LCR of 130% and NSFR of 111%; the fully phased-in gap is closing as the output floor steps toward 72.5% in January 2029, and a separate TLAC requirement (proposed at 14% of RWA, 5-year phase-in) was put out for consultation in May 2026 but is not yet in force.

- Regulatory minima as described by OCBC: "Since 1 January 2019, the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) has fully phased-in the Basel III capital adequacy ratio requirements under the MAS Notice 637. Under this framework, Singapore-incorporated banks are required to meet minimum Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1), Tier 1, and total capital adequacy ratios (CAR) of 6.5%, 8.0%, and 10.0%, respectively... a Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) of 2.5 percentage points above the minimum capital adequacy requirements was introduced. Including the CCB, Singapore-incorporated banks are required to meet CET1 CAR, Tier 1 CAR and Total CAR of 9.0%, 10.5% and 12.5%, respectively"; the final Basel III reforms "include revisions to the credit, market and operational risk standards and the introduction of an output floor transitional arrangement"; the Countercyclical Buffer is "generally in the range of 0% to 2.5% of risk-weighted assets" and "is not an ongoing requirement"; insurance subsidiaries are deducted from capital rather than consolidated for CAR — [OCBC Annual Report 2025, Capital Management pp.74-75](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/investors/annual-reports/2025/2025-annual-report-en.pdf)

- Output-floor phase-in under MAS's implementation circular: 50% from 1 July 2024, 55% from January 2025, 60% from January 2026, 65% from January 2027, 70% from January 2028 and 72.5% from 1 January 2029; the revised market-risk capital and disclosure requirements took effect 1 January 2025 — [Allen & Gledhill note on the MAS circular](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/24869/mas-issues-circular-on-implementation-timeline-for-the-final-basel-iii-reforms-in-singapore); [Allen & Gledhill note on the original consultation](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/10957/mas-issues-consultation-paper-on-proposed-implementation-of-the-final-basel-iii-reforms-in-singapore)
- S&P Global reported that the final Basel III implementation lifted Singapore banks' reported capital ratios by about 1.5 to 2 percentage points from 1 July 2024 (transitional basis) — [S&P Global Market Intelligence, Dec 2024](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2024/12/singapore-banks-capital-equity-tier-1-ratios-climb-due-to-basel-iii-reforms-86707994)
- TLAC: on 13 May 2026 MAS issued Consultation Paper P011-2026 proposing TLAC requirements for D-SIBs (comments closed 12 June 2026). Proposals: local-bank D-SIBs to hold external TLAC of 14% of RWA (versus the FSB's 18% for G-SIBs); no TLAC leverage-ratio backstop; foreign-bank D-SIBs to hold internal TLAC equal to 75-90% of a standalone requirement calibrated at 18% RWA (G-SIB subsidiaries) or 14% RWA (others); "CET1 Capital that is being used to meet the capital conservation buffer cannot be used to also meet TLAC requirements" and capital held against the CCyB ("currently 0%") also cannot count; eligible instruments must be fully paid, unsecured, remaining maturity of at least one year, Singapore-law or enforceable recognition of MAS resolution powers, with a contractual or statutory write-down/conversion trigger; instruments sold to Singapore retail investors cannot count; D-SIBs would get "5 years to meet their TLAC requirements, starting from the date of notification"; disclosure of TLAC as a ratio of RWA quarterly or semi-annually; and amendments to Part 7 of the FSMA to give MAS the power — [MAS Consultation Paper P011-2026 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/publications/consultations/rso/consultation-paper-on-proposed-tlac-framework-for-dsibs-in-singapore.pdf); [Allen & Gledhill summary, 25 May 2026](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/publication/articles/32984/mas-consults-on-proposed-total-loss-absorbing-capacity-requirements-for-domestic-systemically-important-banks-in-singapore)
- OCBC's S$2.5 billion capital return (announced February 2025) comprises special dividends of 10% of core net profit for FY2024 and FY2025 plus share buybacks over 2025-2026; 13.6 million treasury shares were cancelled under it in 2025; FY25 total dividend was 99 cents (60% payout including the 16-cent special) and the 1H26 interim was 47 cents (50% payout, S$2.11 billion) — [OCBC Annual Report 2025, p.74](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/investors/annual-reports/2025/2025-annual-report-en.pdf); [OCBC 1H26 Media Release](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/investors/quarterly-results/2026/OCBC%201H26%20Media%20Release%20Financial%20Highlights.pdf)
- OCBC is rated Aa1 by Moody's and AA- by Fitch and S&P (as stated in the FY25 deck, Feb 2026) — [OCBC FY25 Results Presentation](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/investors/quarterly-results/2025/ocbc%20fy25%20results%20presentation.pdf)

### Inferences
- The transitional-to-fully-phased-in gap narrowed from 1.8 points (Dec 2025: 16.9% vs 15.1%) to 1.5 points (Sep 2026: 15.5% vs 14.0%), consistent with the output floor stepping from 55% to 60% in January 2026; further steps in 2027-2029 will keep compressing reported CET1 toward the fully phased-in figure, so OCBC's 14% fully phased-in target is the binding internal constraint, not the 9.0% regulatory all-in minimum.
- The 1.4-point year-on-year CET1 decline to September 2026 reflects 14% loan growth (RWA S$249bn to S$270bn by June 2026), the special dividend/buyback programme and FY25 final dividend, rather than losses; OCBC still has roughly 5 points of headroom over the 9.0% all-in requirement and about 5.7 points over the IWST-2025 adverse-scenario trough of 9.8% for the D-SIB aggregate.
- If the TLAC proposal is adopted at 14% of RWA with CCB-funded CET1 excluded, OCBC's current regulatory capital (Total CAR 18.3% at Sep 2026) would already cover the headline figure arithmetically, but eligibility tests (e.g. Tier 2 amortisation, retail-holder exclusion, structured notes excluded) mean some issuance of senior non-preferred-style instruments is likely over the 5-year window; MAS has not yet published a response or final rules.

### Gaps
- Pillar 3 report (FY2025/1H2026) with the RWA split and the IRRBB standardised tables could not be located (OCBC's investor-information page is image-heavy and the Pillar 3 links were not extractable), so the output-floor-driven RWA add-on in S$ is not quantified here.

- No MAS response to the TLAC consultation had been published as of 7 November 2026 (none found on the MAS consultation listing); effective date therefore unknown.

## Key question 2: Credit risk (loan book by industry and geography, Hong Kong/mainland China CRE, Singapore property, oil and gas/shipping history, SME exposure, NPL and coverage history, credit-cost guidance)

OCBC's S$372 billion loan book (Sep 2026) is 44% Singapore and 21% Greater China by credit-risk residency, with "building and construction" (property-related) the largest industry at 28% and housing loans at 21% (Dec 2025); Greater China is the weak spot (NPL ratio 2.0% at Dec 2025, NPAs up 41% in 2025, driven by two corporate real-estate downgrades in 4Q25) but group asset quality is strong (NPL 0.8%, NPA coverage 168% at Sep 2026) and management guides 2026 credit costs of 15-20bps after 14bps in 9M26.

### Inferences
- The combination of "building and construction" at 28% of loans, S$1.7bn of NPLs in that sector (54% of group NPLs), and Greater China NPLs rising 41% in 2025 identifies Hong Kong/China commercial real estate as the single most important credit sensitivity; 2026 data (falling NPAs, 6bps 3Q26 credit cost) suggest the 2024-2025 CRE cycle has been provisioned through rather than resolved.
- The S$20bn "offshore China" bucket plus S$9bn onshore and S$34bn Hong Kong (S$63bn, Dec 2025) is the number to stress against mainland property deflation and HK office capital-value declines; the HK rental recovery in 2026 supports debt service but JLL's capital-value forecast implies collateral LTVs remain under pressure.
- Mortgage risk is low (housing NPL 0.5% at OCBC, 0.3% system-wide) and Singapore prices are still rising, so Singapore property is a volume driver rather than a credit risk; Indonesia (NPL 2.5-2.6%) and the 2026 "Indonesia" overlay are the next watch-items after Greater China.

## Key question 6: Conduct and operational risk (2021 SMS phishing scam and MAS capital add-on, Shared Responsibility Framework, 2023 S$3 billion money-laundering case and 2025 penalties, 2025-2026 enforcement, outages)

### Takeaway
OCBC's conduct record since 2021 comprises the December 2021 SMS phishing wave (790 customers, S$13.7 million, fully reimbursed as goodwill) which led MAS to impose about S$330 million of additional capital in May 2022; a S$600,000 AML penalty in June 2023 (Wirecard-related); and no penalty in the July 2025 S$27.45 million round linked to the 2023 S$3 billion laundering case — OCBC was not among the nine institutions fined, and MAS's quarterly enforcement summaries for Q1-Q3 2026 contain no OCBC action. - Shared Responsibility Framework: MAS Guidelines published 24 October 2024, implemented 16 December 2024, applying to full banks (local and branch) and major payment institutions, covering "a defined scope of phishing scams" and requiring "payouts to affected scam victims where these duties are breached" — [MAS Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (page)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework). The responsible FI's assessed duties (para 4.2) are: (1) a cooling-off period "of at least 12 hours where high-risk activities cannot be performed" after digital-token activation or new-device login; (2) real-time notification alerts for token activation, new-device login and high-risk activities; (3) real-time outgoing-transaction alerts; (4) a 24/7 reporting channel and self-service "kill switch" to block the account; (5) real-time fraud surveillance that, where "a protected account is rapidly drained of a material sum" (more than 50% of balance within 24 hours), blocks or holds the transaction for at least 24 hours with notification. Under para 6.2 the FI bears the loss "if the loss arises from any non-compliance by the responsible FI with any duty", and under 6.5 bears it even if the telco also failed; the telco bears loss only where the FI met all duties and the telco did not; account holders must report within 30 calendar days and furnish supporting information (e.g. police report) within 3 calendar days; both FI and telco need investigation governance "involving representatives who are independent from business units" and must retain logs — [Guidelines on Shared Responsibility Framework (PDF, IMDA copy)](https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/regulations-and-licensing/regulations/consultations/2024/shared-responsibility-framework-for-phishing-scams/guidelines-on-shared-responsibility-framework.pdf); MAS and IMDA said they "will not introduce any liability cap" and expect "full payouts in the event of breach of duties", and FIs were given a 6-month transition after 16 December 2024 for the real-time transaction-notification duty — [A&O Shearman note](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-fintech-and-digital-assets/combatting-payment-account-fraud-singapores-shared-responsibility-framework); [HSF Kramer note, Dec 2024](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/data/2024-posts/financial-institutions-and-telcos-required-to-share-responsibility-for-phishing-scams-in-singapore); [Fintech News Singapore](https://fintechnews.sg/105280/regtech/singapore-shared-responsibility-framework-starts/)

### Inferences
- OCBC's regulatory scar tissue is scam-handling and operational response (2021-2022), not AML (its 2023 fine was small and it was absent from the 2025 round); any vendor platform that touches customer channels will be assessed against the SRF duties and the FSM-N05 availability thresholds first.

## Key question 7: Rules a third-party analytics/AI vendor must satisfy to work with OCBC (Banking Act s47/s47A, Notice 658 and Outsourcing Guidelines, proposed TPRM Guidelines, TRM Guidelines and FSM-N05/N06, incident reporting, BCM, PDPA, FEAT/Veritas, AI MRM paper, the new AI Risk Management Guidelines, cloud, research/financial-advice rules) and what each requires in practice

### Takeaway
A vendor to OCBC sits inside a layered regime: the Banking Act's secrecy rule (s47) and MAS Notice 658 (binding since 11 December 2024) govern any service that receives customer information, requiring contractual confidentiality, need-to-know access, MAS/bank audit rights, deletion on exit, sub-contracting consent and semi-annual registration; the TRM Guidelines and FSM-N05/N06 impose availability (4 hours downtime a year, 4-hour RTO), cyber-hygiene and secure-development expectations that the bank must be able to enforce on the vendor; and, from 7 October 2026, MAS's Guidelines on AI Risk Management treat any AI the vendor supplies or embeds as "third-party AI" that OCBC must inventory, risk-rate (Impact/Complexity/Reliance), test on its own data, keep under human oversight proportionate to materiality, and document (including prompt/output logging for GenAI) — with a 12-month deadline (October 2027) for governance/inventory and 24 months (October 2028) for full lifecycle controls. A research-publishing feature additionally engages the Financial Advisers Act (issuing research reports is a financial advisory service, with conflict-of-interest guidelines FAA-G13 and recommendation rules FAA-N16), and the PDPA's 10%-of-turnover penalty regime applies to any personal data.

### Cited Findings

Banking secrecy and outsourcing (binding rules)
- Section 47 of the Banking Act 1970 prohibits a bank or its officers from disclosing customer information except as the Act's Third Schedule permits; Part I recipients may on-disclose, Part II recipients may not; MAS confirms "the disclosure of customer information to any contractor directly engaged by the bank (i.e. a service provider) to perform outsourced functions continues to be allowed, as set out in the Third Schedule of the BA", but onward disclosure to a sub-contractor requires the customer's written consent — "it is not a new requirement" — and access by a service provider's affiliates or representatives "would be considered an instance of disclosure of customer information by the bank and the bank should ensure any such disclosure complies with... Section 47" — [MAS FAQ on Notices 658 and 1121, 11 Dec 2024 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/faqs/bd/faq-on-outsourcing-guidelines/faq-for-notice-658-and-1121_11dec2024.pdf); [Singapore Law Watch, Information and Communications Technology Law ch.22](https://www.singaporelawwatch.sg/About-Singapore-Law/Commercial-Law/ch-22-banking-and-finance)
- MAS Notice 658 "Management of Outsourced Relevant Services for Banks" was issued 11 December 2023 under section 47A of the Banking Act and took effect 11 December 2024; it applies to all banks in Singapore — [MAS Notice 658 page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/notices/notice-658). Key obligations in the text: (a) a register of all ongoing outsourced relevant services and all outsourced relevant services involving disclosure of customer information, "updated promptly" and submitted "semi-annually and at any time upon request" (para 3) — within 15 business days of 30 June and 31 December per Circular BD 08/2024; (b) before a "material ongoing outsourced relevant service" (MOORS), a documented evaluation of the provider's ability to perform, its financial strength, and (where overseas and customer information is involved) the laws of that place and the provider's confidentiality track record, re-performed "no later than 24 months" after onboarding and thereafter at a board-approved frequency (para 5); (c) a MOORS cannot be sub-contracted where customer information is disclosed unless the customer has consented in writing (para 6.1), the provider must notify the bank before/within a reasonable time of engaging a sub-contractor, and the bank must document its sub-contracting assessment (para 6.3); (d) the outsourcing agreement must require the provider to protect "the confidentiality and integrity of all information of the bank in its custody", to access or process customer information "only to the extent that is necessary", not to "disclose any customer information of the bank to any third party unless compelled by law" (with notification to the bank), to allow "the Authority, or an auditor appointed by the Authority... to audit the books, systems and premises of the service provider", to give the bank termination rights (including on any failure to safeguard customer information), and on termination to ensure customer information is "deleted, destroyed or rendered unusable as soon as possible" (para 7.1); (e) independent audits of each MOORS "at least once every three years", with reports available to MAS on request (para 9) — pooled audits or third-party certifications (e.g. ABS OSPAR reports) are acceptable "as long as the audit reports fulfill the requirements"; (f) notification to MAS "as soon as possible" of adverse developments and of any decision to terminate (para 7-11); (g) for overseas MOORS, written policies on foreign-supervisor access requests and "a written undertaking to notify the Authority in writing of any disclosure of Customer Information to the supervisory authority, within 14 working days"; (h) Section C applies to any outsourced relevant service involving disclosure of customer information regardless of materiality (due diligence on confidentiality safeguards, employee confidentiality obligations, ongoing satisfaction, documentation); (i) Annex C lists "Public cloud services including, but not limited to, software-as-a-service, platform-as-a-service and infrastructure-as-a-service" as outsourced relevant services; Annex D exempts services where the provider has no access to confidential or customer information — [MAS Notice 658 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/notices/bd/notice-658/mas-notice-658---4.pdf); [MAS FAQ on Notices 658/1121 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/faqs/bd/faq-on-outsourcing-guidelines/faq-for-notice-658-and-1121_11dec2024.pdf)
- "Material" is defined by whether failure, unauthorised access or breach "will materially affect adversely or is likely to materially affect adversely" the bank's business, customers, or its ability to comply with law — [MAS Notice 658 (PDF), para 2](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/notices/bd/notice-658/mas-notice-658---4.pdf)
- MAS Guidelines on Outsourcing (Banks), issued 11 December 2023 and effective 11 December 2024, to be read with the Notice: due diligence should resemble "a credit assessment of the viability of the service provider" (audited financials, sponsors), cover security and internal controls, "audit coverage", technology-risk capability, DR track record, reliance on sub-contractors and insurance; agreements should contain clauses allowing the bank "to conduct audits on the service provider and its sub-contractors, whether by its internal or external auditors, or by agents appointed by the Bank" and to obtain copies of the provider's own audit reports; banks should disclose customer information "only on a need-to-know basis", address "the party liable for losses in the event of a breach of security or confidentiality", and require providers to disclose confidentiality breaches; banks "should not enter into outsourcing arrangements with service providers in jurisdictions where prompt access to information by MAS... may be impeded"; cloud (Annex 2) requires the provider to "clearly identify and segregate customer data using strong physical or logical controls" with access controls that "survive the tenure of the contract"; outsourcing all or substantially all of a risk-management or internal-control function is automatically material; non-observance can lead MAS to require "pre-notification of new material ongoing outsourced relevant services" — [MAS Guidelines on Outsourcing (Banks) (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/guidelines/bd/guidelines-on-outsourcing/guidelines-on-outsourcing-banks-updated.pdf); [MAS Guidelines page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-outsourcing-banks)
- Proposed Guidelines on Third-Party Risk Management (Consultation Paper P004-2026, 6 March 2026, closed 20 April 2026; "This consultation is closed", no response or final guidelines listed): the Guidelines "supersede the Guidelines on Outsourcing (Banks) and Guidelines on Outsourcing (Financial Institutions other than Banks), and expand the application of the relevant expectations currently imposed on outsourced services to all third-party services"; "For banks and merchant banks, MAS Notices 658 and 1121 will remain in effect and continue to set baseline requirements"; FIs would submit a single register of third-party arrangements semi-annually (template in Annex B, including material sub-contractors and service locations), map dependencies and concentration risk, perform risk assessment, due diligence, contracting, onboarding/monitoring and termination controls; audit frequency of material arrangements to be board-approved; service-provider agreements should give "the FI's and MAS' right, or that of an auditor appointed by the FI or MAS, to (i) audit and inspect the service provider; (ii) access relevant information and premises", rights over data, software, models and IP on exit, and notification of adverse developments; FIs should ensure the provider notifies them "in writing prior to the engagement of a material sub-contractor"; pass-through sub-contracting is permitted but must be risk-assessed; exit plans "regularly updated and tested"; FMIs and utilities exempt; cloud guidance (Annex 4) adds portability/interoperability to mitigate "vendor lock-in or concentration" — [MAS consultation page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/consultations/1/consultation-paper-on-proposed-guidelines-on-third-party-risk-management); [Consultation Paper P004-2026 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/publications/consultations/bd/2026/consultation-paper---tprmg.pdf); [MAS third-party risk management focus page (updated 6 Mar 2026)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/third-party-risk-management); law-firm context: [HSF Kramer, 2026](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/fsrandcorpcrime/2026-posts/extending-the-regulatory-perimeter-beyond-outsourcing-mas-proposed-third-party-risk); [Rajah & Tann, 2026](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/viewpoints/mas-proposes-new-third-party-risk-management-guidelines-to-cover-all-third-party-services/)
- MAS's 2022 Information Paper on Operational Risk Management – Management of Third-Party Arrangements (thematic inspections of banks) remains a reference for supervisory expectations — [MAS information paper, 2022](https://www.mas.gov.sg/publications/monographs-or-information-paper/2022/operational-risk-management---management-of-third-party-arrangements)

Technology risk, cyber hygiene, incident reporting, business continuity
- MAS Technology Risk Management Guidelines (18 January 2021): the FI "should assess and manage its exposure to technology risks that may affect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the IT systems and data at the third party before entering into a contractual agreement" and ensure "the third party employs a high standard of care and diligence in protecting data confidentiality and integrity as well as ensuring system resilience" (3.4); vendor evaluation standards and due diligence "commensurate with the criticality" (5.3.1), assessment of the vendor's "software development and quality assurance practices" with vendor access "controlled and monitored" (5.3.2), and a source-code escrow assessment (5.3.4); policies for review and testing of third-party and open-source code and tracking of their vulnerabilities (6.1.3-6.1.4); secure coding, source-code review and application security testing, applied in Agile/DevSecOps with segregation of duties (6.1-6.3); for APIs, a vetting process for third parties "taking into account... cyber security posture, industry reputation and track record", risk assessment before connection, protection of API keys/tokens with expiry, strong encryption, pre-production security testing, logging of third-party access sessions, real-time monitoring and prompt key revocation (6.4); data-loss-prevention policies, encryption of confidential data at rest and in transit, controls on unauthorised cloud storage/file sharing (11.1); multi-factor authentication for sensitive functions and remote access (9.1.5, 9.3); vulnerability assessment, black-box and grey-box penetration testing, cyber exercises and adversarial attack simulation (13) — [MAS TRM Guidelines 2021 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/regulations-and-financial-stability/regulatory-and-supervisory-framework/risk-management/trm-guidelines-18-january-2021.pdf); [MAS TRM Guidelines page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/technology-risk-management-guidelines)
- MAS Notice FSM-N05 Technology Risk Management (issued 9 May 2024 under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2022, effective 10 May 2024, replacing the cancelled Notice 644): banks must identify "critical systems", keep "maximum unscheduled downtime for each critical system... [to] not exceed a total of 4 hours within any period of 12 months", set an RTO "of not more than 4 hours" validated at least annually, notify MAS "not later than 1 hour" after discovering a relevant incident, submit a root-cause and impact report within 14 days, and "implement IT controls to protect customer information from unauthorised access or disclosure" — [MAS Notice FSM-N05 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/notices/trpd/notice-fsm-n05/mas-notice-fsm-n05.pdf); [MAS Notice FSM-N05 page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/notices/notice-fsm-n05)
- MAS Notice FSM-N06 Cyber Hygiene (issued 9 May 2024, effective 10 May 2024, replacing Notice 655): secure every administrative account; apply security patches "within a timeframe that is commensurate with the risks"; maintain "a written set of security standards for every system"; network-perimeter controls; malware protection on every system; multi-factor authentication for all administrative accounts on critical systems and "all accounts on any system used by the relevant entity to access customer information through the internet". A bank need not comply only where it "cannot exercise direct control over the system", "cannot exercise indirect control... by requiring the system provider to ensure compliance" and cannot reasonably procure an alternative provider — [MAS Notice FSM-N06 (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas-media-library/regulation/notices/trpd/notice-fsm-n06/mas-notice-fsm-n06.pdf); [MAS Notice FSM-N06 page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/notices/notice-fsm-n06)
- Incident reporting: MAS's Circular on Financial Institution Incident Reporting (16 December 2025) requires FIs, from 1 February 2026, to report reportable incidents via the updated FI Incident Reporting Template on the MAS-Tx platform "as soon as possible, but no later than the timeline prescribed" in the applicable notices — [MAS circular page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/circulars/circular-on-financial-institution-incident-reporting); [MAS instructions page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/forms-and-templates/instructions-on-incident-notification-and-reporting-to-mas)
- MAS Guidelines on Business Continuity Management (6 June 2022; FIs to comply and set a BCM audit plan within 12 months, first audit within 24 months): identify critical business services and set a Service Recovery Time Objective (SRTO) for each; map end-to-end dependencies "including those involving third parties"; "put in place measures that enable third parties to meet the SRTOs", such as SLAs, reviewing third parties' BCPs, audits (ISO 22301 certification audits may be relied on if independent and adequately scoped) or joint tests; plans for "unforeseen disruption, failure or termination of third-party arrangements"; manage concentration risk when "several of its critical business services and/or functions are outsourced to a single service provider"; BCM audit at least every three years; annual senior-management attestation to the Board (available to MAS on request) — [MAS BCM Guidelines (PDF)](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/regulations-and-financial-stability/regulatory-and-supervisory-framework/risk-management/bcm-guidelines/guidelines-on-bcm-june-2022.pdf); [MAS BCM page](https://www.mas.gov.sg/regulation/guidelines/guidelines-on-business-continuity-management). OCBC's own BCM programme "identifies Critical Business Services, sets Service Recovery Time Objectives, and maps supporting processes, systems, third-party service providers" with annual exercises, and its Third-Party Risk Management programme "includes a stringent onboarding process for third-party service providers, ongoing monitoring and periodic due diligence assessments" — [OCBC Annual Report 2025, p.90](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/investors/annual-reports/2025/2025-annual-report-en.pdf)

Personal data
- PDPA (as amended 2020): consent, purpose limitation and notification, access/correction, accuracy, protection ("reasonable security arrangements"), retention limitation, transfer limitation (overseas recipients must give "comparable protection" via contract, consent or prescribed exceptions), accountability (DPO), and data portability (commencement pending). Data intermediaries processing under a written contract are exempt from most obligations but "remain directly liable for retention and protection obligations" and "must notify the organization without undue delay" of a breach; organisations must have written agreements with intermediaries. Breach notification: assess promptly; notify the PDPC "no later than 3 calendar days" after assessing a breach as notifiable (significant harm, or 500 or more individuals), and notify affected individuals where there is significant harm. Penalties: up to 10% of annual Singapore turnover for organisations with turnover above S$10 million, otherwise up to S$1 million. Private organisations must stop using NRIC numbers for authentication by 31 December 2026 — [DLA Piper Data Protection Laws of the World: Singapore](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/index.html?t=law&c=SG)
- OCBC runs a "Data Protection Honour Roll" and Data Loss Prevention controls; staff access is "governed by a strict need-to-know principle" — [OCBC Annual Report 2025, pp.21, 92](https://www.ocbc.com/iwov-resources/sg/ocbc/gbc/pdf/investors/annual-reports/2025/2025-annual-report-en.pdf)

### Inferences
- Practical design requirements for a vendor platform at OCBC, derived from the above: (1) data residency is not mandated by MAS per se, but offshore processing of customer information triggers Notice 658 Section C/para 5.1(c) due diligence on local law, policies on foreign-supervisor access and a 14-working-day MAS notification undertaking, so Singapore-hosted (or at least Singapore-controllable) deployment removes a compliance workstream; (2) the contract must grant OCBC, MAS and MAS-appointed auditors audit/inspection rights, need-to-know access, no onward disclosure, breach notification, deletion on exit, prior notification of sub-contractors (and no customer-information sub-contracting without customer consent), notification before AI is introduced or changed, performance guarantees and exit/portability (the proposed TPRM Guidelines add rights over data, models and IP on termination); (3) controls must let OCBC meet FSM-N05/N06 "indirect control": patching SLAs, written security standards, MFA for admin and internet access to customer data, DLP, encryption, API-key management and logging, penetration-test evidence, and availability consistent with a 4-hour RTO; (4) under the AIRG the vendor must supply enough transparency (model documentation, training-data provenance, evaluation results on OCBC's own data, change notifications, prompt/output logs for GenAI, explainability artefacts) for OCBC to inventory the use case, rate its materiality and validate it independently; research or advice outputs reaching clients will be rated higher-materiality ("provision of financial advisory... services") and will need human review before publication; (5) any personal data processing makes the vendor a PDPA data intermediary with direct protection/retention duties and breach notification to OCBC.
- Because OCBC is an exempt financial adviser, research published under OCBC's name through a vendor tool is OCBC's regulated activity, so FAA-G13 conflict controls, FAA-N16 suitability rules (where recommendations are made) and the SFA representation rules bind the content workflow; a vendor publishing research in its own name to Singapore retail investors would itself need FAA authorisation unless an exemption applies.
- The AIRG deadlines (governance/inventory by ~7 October 2027; full lifecycle controls by ~7 October 2028; high-risk use cases immediately) create a near-term buying window for tooling that produces AI inventories, materiality assessments and evaluation evidence.
