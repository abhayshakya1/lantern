## Key question 1: OCBC annual metrics FY2022, FY2023, FY2024, FY2025

### Takeaway
OCBC's net profit rose from S$5.75bn (FY2022) to records of S$7.02bn (FY2023) and S$7.59bn (FY2024), then slipped 2% to S$7.42bn in FY2025 as NIM fell 29bps to 1.91%; FY2025 nonetheless set records for total income (S$14.6bn), profit before tax (S$9.12bn), wealth AUM (S$343bn) and non-interest income (S$5.46bn), while dividends rose from 68 cents (FY22) to 101 cents (FY24) and 99 cents (FY25) under a 60% total payout and the S$2.5bn two-year capital return plan.

### Inferences
- The FY2025 profit decline was entirely rate-driven: NII fell S$605m while every non-interest line, operating profit before allowances (flat at S$8.73bn) and PBT (+2%) improved; the net-profit drop reflects the higher tax charge (Pillar Two).
- Non-interest income's share of total income rose from 28.6% (FY23) to 37.4% (FY25), making OCBC's earnings progressively more fee/wealth/insurance-driven and less NIM-sensitive.
- CET1 on the fully phased-in basis has been managed down from 15.3% (Dec 24) toward the 14% target through the capital return plan, consistent with management's stated intention to lift ROE by ~1ppt.

### Gaps
- OCBC does not publish a single five-year summary table in the documents retrieved; the FY2022 column mixes the originally reported basis and the SFRS(I) 17 restatement, so the report writer should pick one basis (recommend the restated FY22 net profit of S$5,526m for like-for-like comparison with FY23+).
- FY2025 total weighted new sales for GEH and the FY2022/FY2023 fully-phased-in CET1 equivalents are not disclosed in the sources used (the final Basel III basis only applied from 1 July 2024).
- Share buyback executed amounts (shares repurchased and S$ spent) per year were not found in the retrieved documents; only the "about S$800m remaining" statement of August 2026 was located.

### Inferences
- The 2026 guidance trajectory (loan growth: mid-single → high-single/low-double → low-double digit; credit costs: 20–25 → 15–20bps; total income: "stable to growing" → "at least mid-single-digit") shows management upgrading as the year progressed; 9M26 actuals (loans +14%, income +7%, credit costs 14bps, CIR 39.2%) already sit at or beyond the upgraded ranges.
- The rate assumption embedded in guidance flipped from a Fed cut (May 2026) to a Fed hike (Nov 2026), consistent with the Middle East conflict/energy-price narrative in the CEO messages; OCBC describes itself as positively geared to higher rates once assets reprice.
- Quarterly Group profit volatility in 2026 is driven mainly by GEH's mark-to-market investment results (GEH contribution S$471m in 2Q26 vs S$147m in 3Q26), while Banking Operations profit has risen for three consecutive quarters to a record S$1.90bn.

### Gaps
- 2Q25 net profit YoY change, 2Q25 loan growth and 2Q25 quarterly WM fee amount were not stated in the retrieved sources (2Q25 WM fees of roughly S$324m can be inferred from "2Q26 S$470m, +45% YoY" in Kopi Notes, but this is not a primary-source figure).
- OCBC does not disclose a quarterly geographic or business-segment P&L; segment PBT is disclosed only at half-year and full-year.
- The 1H26 CEO deck could not be text-extracted (image-only), so the August 2026 guidance is cited from secondary coverage of the call rather than the slide itself.
- Exact NIM guidance for FY2026 was never expressed as a number (only NII direction and rate assumptions).

- On 9M26 numbers, OCBC's profit growth (+10%) is the strongest of the three (DBS +2%, UOB +27% off a provision-depressed base), and OCBC is the only one whose 3Q26 result beat consensus (DBS and UOB both missed).
- OCBC's NIM (1.66%) is now the lowest of the three on a group basis, but its loan growth (+14% YoY) is the fastest, so NII is rising YoY (+4%) while DBS's rose 4% and UOB's 1%.
- OCBC's transitional CET1 (15.5%) is the highest and its NPL ratio (0.8%) the lowest; UOB's CET1 (14.6%) and NPL (1.5%) are the weakest, which, with its 1.1x P/B, frames UOB as the laggard and DBS as the premium name in the comparison.
